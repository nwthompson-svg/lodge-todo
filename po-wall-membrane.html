The job

Cut the black Traspir for the plant room walls: two rows all the way round, one above the other. Each row is three pieces, each running from one window frame, round the corner(s), to the next frame.

Then reroll the two long back pieces (B1, B2) so they go straight up.

Before you cut

  • Check the roll first. There must be a whole roll left for this job, with at least 41.5 m on it (a full roll is 50 m). If there isn't, stop and message Neil before cutting anything.
  • No spare (Neil). The lengths are exact. Don't add any.
  • Each length = frame to frame round the corners, plus 75 at each end that laps onto the frame. The 75 is measured from the edge of the frame (the frame sits right at the edge of the opening, so it's the same line).
  • Measure each run first with a tape round the wood fibre, frame edge to frame edge (the edge of the opening if a frame isn't in yet). It should match the frame to frame figure in the cut list within 10. If it's out by more, cut to your tape + 150 and message Neil.
  • Wood fibre not on a wall yet? Measure on the CLT instead: A 3699, B 11313, C 4814. Cut the same lengths.
  • Full roll width (1500). No ripping.
  • All six come off that one roll. Cut B1 and B2 first. Never join a piece.
  • Label every piece as you cut it, on the back (the side without the writing): name and row, e.g. B1 BOTTOM.

3. Which way round
  • Stand outside facing the wall: every piece starts at the frame on your left and runs to the right, round the corner(s).
  • Writing faces out.
  • The writing of both rows goes in the middle of the wall, where they meet: bottom row = writing along its top edge, top row = writing along its bottom edge.
  • Bottom row goes up first; the top row overlaps it in the middle, like the Lodge.
  • 75 onto the frame at both ends.
  • Check as it goes up: measured from the start end, the corners land at A 3143, B 1282 and 9802, C 827.
4. Rerolling the long ones (B1, B2)

So each one comes off the roll the right way round, writing out and writing edge in the middle.

  1. Lay the piece out flat, writing side UP, on clean, dry ground.
  2. Stand on one long side, as if you were looking at the wall. Turn it so the writing edge is AWAY from you for B1 (bottom row) and NEAR you for B2 (top row).
  3. The LEFT end is now the START. Write START on the back at that end. Then tick the two corners on the edge, writing side, measured from the START end: 1282 and 9802 (chalk or a silver pen), so you can see them land on the corners as it goes up.
  4. Go to the right-hand end and roll it up towards the START end, rolling it over on top of itself so the writing ends up on the inside. Keep the edges lined up so it rolls straight (a straight batten in the middle helps).
  5. The START end is now the loose end on the outside. Write UP on the end of the roll that was away from you.

Putting it up: stand the roll on end, UP at the top, at the frame where the piece starts. The loose end goes on the wall with 75 onto the frame, writing facing you. Staple it, then roll it along to the right, pressed against the wall.

Why it works: laid out like that, the piece is the wall laid down flat, so it rolls back out exactly as it lay.

5. Heights on the wall

Measure up from the underside of the floor joists (the bottom of the wood fibre). The battens are given by the bottom of each batten.

Before you start

  • Check the wall: underside of the joists to the top of the roof panel should be 2780. If it's out by more than 10, message Neil before marking anything.
  • Mark the lines all the way round at the same heights, so the rows meet at the corners.
  • Staple only where a batten will cover it. The top row's top edge is stapled between 2640 and 2680 (R5 covers it). No tape on the top edge.
  • The top row goes over the roof's silver flap and under the roof rubber. If the silver flap isn't down the wall yet, staple the top row only under the R3 and R4 lines and leave its top 150 free.
  • If the tarp frame posts are still on the front or back wall, they come off first.
