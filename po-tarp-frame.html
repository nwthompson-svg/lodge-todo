The job

Five frames that stand over the plant room roof and hold a tarp while we work up there. Each frame is a tall front post (bifold side), a short back post and one sloping rafter. Six rows of battens (purlins) run along the top under the tarp.

Tomorrow is cutting and prep only. The plant room isn't up yet. The kit waits until the roof panels are on.

Before you cut

Measure a 4x2 across its wide face first.

This page assumes it's 95. If it's the full 100, message Neil. Carry on cutting: none of the lengths change.

  • Every cut is square. No angles anywhere on this job.
  • CRS timber (came Monday 19/10): 8 × 4x2 at 4.8 m, 14 battens at 4.8 m, 2 × 2x2 at 4.8 m. Leave the two 2x2s: they're Neil's spares.
  • Back posts and spacers come from the leftover 2x4 and 2x2 from the steel-leg bracing.
  • Mark every post from its top end. The top end sets the rafter height, so a back post that's a bit long or short just runs further down the wall or finishes higher up it. That's why "or close length" is fine.
  • Nothing gets sanded up on the building. Any rounding is done here, on the trestles.

4. Going up (later)

When the plant room roof panels are on. Section across the building through one frame:

posts + rafterpurlinsfoamtarp

Where the frames go

Rafter centres at 200 · 2125 · 4050 · 6175 · 8300, measured from the outside of the plant end (the end with the narrow window). Each post sits on the plant-end side of its rafter.

Check the middle front post is at least 100 clear of the bifold. If it isn't, put that post on the other side of its rafter. The rafter stays at 4050 so the purlin joint still lands on it.

Order of work

  1. Mark the walls: at each post position, front and back, the spacer goes 220 to 700 below the top of the roof panel. The top 220 stays clear for the EPDM.
  2. Spacers on: pad against the wall, 3 × 6×140 on the marks.
  3. Posts on: front posts on the bifold wall, back posts on the back wall. Roof-line mark level with the top of the roof panel, plumb, then 3 × HBS 160 on the × marks.
  4. Rafters on (stand on the roof: chest height at the front, waist height at the back). High end at the front, post zones on the posts. Set the rafter's top edge level with the post top at the corner where the rafter is lowest, so nothing sticks up. 2 × 6×90 each end.
  5. Corner braces where Neil wants them, from the rafter offcuts.
  6. Purlins on top: one A and one B per row, butted on the middle rafter, ends flush with the ends of the building, on the rafter marks. One 5×60 per crossing, both ends at the joint.
  7. Diagonals under the rafters, one in each end bay, corner to corner. Cut to fit. 2 × 5×60 each end.
  8. Foam blocks on all ten rafter ends.
  9. Tarp over, pulled tight front to back. Bottom edges battened to the front and back walls with the spruce 4x1s about 600 apart. Tighten it again after the first rain. Slacken or drop it before a big storm.
